# Component card: the coupon studio and the workspace | version 0.11 | updated 2026-11-19

## Purpose and intended use

The coupon FEA workspace at `/workspace/fea` (ADR-0062; `/chat`, `/chat/view`, `/studio` and `/studio/view` redirect there with their query), where an engineer states a coupon test, checks its setup, solves it and reads the result in a 3D view, without installing anything (ADR-0051). Three columns: one folding panel per step on the left, the stating steps numbered (1 Test and standard, 2 Project, 3 Geometry, 4 Layup and material, 5 Boundary conditions and loads, 6 Mesh; then Validate, Run, Outputs, and Advanced with every other row), under a line that says what is being set up (the test and the standard it follows, or none); the first step asks for the test and then for one of our standards that covers it, and the steps that depend on the test wait for it and show what the standard asks of their rows (ADR-0062 amendment 1), each editing named rows of the one values file; in the centre a sketch of the typed rows until the check, then the package's preview, then the fields after a run; on the right the coupon assistant, which offers one-click choices, reads a typed line into the rows, names the panel that asks for the next thing, and under each thing to fix on its latest check offers the rows the check names, applied together and checked again (ADR-0062 amendment 2). The front door `/workspace` lists the projects this browser keeps and starts a coupon or a laminate project; `/workspace/laminate` is the laminate workspace (ADR-0060; ADR-0062 part 5): Units, Material, Layup, Loads and Solve panels, the stack drawn ply by ply and the engine's answer in the centre, the laminate assistant beside them; CAD is not a workspace until geometry from a file exists (ADR-0091 amendment 2). It replaces the terminal for the four in-plane coupon tests (unnotched tension and compression, open-hole tension, in-plane shear), the two bending tests (three- and four-point bending, ADR-0053: the span, the overhang and the loading span as rows; the deflection along z, the rotations, the moments, the transverse shear forces and both faces of every ply in the viewer) and the `plate` test (ADR-0054: supports and loads over named regions, stated on the panel or by clicking an element of the model, pressures included). A coupon can be a plastic (ADR-0075): Material kind = isotropic on the Layup step gives its thickness, E and nu in place of the plies, and an unnotched tension coupon can be a dog-bone, its narrow section, fillets and Type IV's outer radius on the Geometry step, sketched as the engine meshes it and sized by ASTM D638-22's Types I to V. The viewer opens in a second window by the job's link, `/workspace/fea/view?job=<id>`.

## What it does and does not compute

Nothing. Every number the page shows is written by the fea package: the setup checklist and the preview (`POST /v1/coupon-jobs/check`), the fields with their display averages, the ranges of every field and the summary (`fields.json`, ADR-0063), and the template explanation (`explanation.json`), read as files of the job (ADR-0050 amendment 1); a laminate problem is solved by the engine through `POST /v1/laminates/solve` (ADR-0060). The page parses text into the values object, picks arrays, maps values to colours and draws (ADR-0001); the sketch draws typed rows only and prints no number it derives. The form's rows are the package's own definition, `lib/coupon-form-definition.json`, exported by `fea/tools/export_form_definition.py` and checked against the package by `fea/tests/test_studio_tool.py`. The explanation of a result may be rephrased by a language model behind a setting, every number checked against the engine's (ADR-0052); the assistants' readers are fixed patterns, not a model.

## Inputs / outputs

Input: a project (`lib/project-store.ts`), kept in this browser under `anisotropy.projects.v1` until the server store of ADR-0062 part 6 exists: for a coupon the rows of the coupon input form (ADR-0046) with the coupon's codes derived from the material, the test and the layup (ADR-0038 amendment 1), typed on the panels, picked from the library, loaded from the public example, read back from a coupon on the machine (`GET /v1/coupons/{name}/values`) or stated to the assistant; for a laminate the draft of the problem, each value with the unit it was typed in. The rows are stated in the unit system the engineer picks (mm-MPa, m-Pa, in-psi or in-ksi; ADR-0061): the labels, the chips, the tape estimate and the library figures the page shows in that system are written by the fea package into `lib/coupon-form-definition.json`, and the page converts nothing; the results, the checklist and the PDF come back in the same system, and the decks stay in mm-MPa (or m-Pa when stated so). Output: the checklist (thirteen items, each passed, warned, failed, missing or pending, with the rows it concerns and each panel's badge the worst of its items; the Test standard item lists each requirement of the standard picked with this coupon's value and what its clause asks, ADR-0072; the Test and standard panel's card shows the same before and after a check, and each later step lists the requirements that name its rows, and the Geometry panel offers the standard's sizes as buttons that copy the engine's figures into the rows), the preview of the model (the mesh with the held tab framed in violet and the pulled tab framed in amber with an arrow, lit for presentation), the job's stages, the fields (displacement, strains, section forces, every ply's stresses in material axes and its von Mises stress, the failure index and strength ratio by criterion; element values or display averages, deformed or not, coloured unlit on the graphics card with bands and an isoline at every legend tick, the legend's ends the package's), the summary (reaction, largest displacement, largest ply stresses, first-ply failure by criterion with the critical element framed in red, and for a coupon under a test standard the quantities the standard reduces a test to: area, stress, the extensometer's strain, the standard's modulus (D3039's chord, D638's modulus of elasticity over the narrow section) beside laminate theory and CalculiX, Poisson's ratio, the net section, the first-ply-failure stress; ADR-0072), the explanation, and links to every file of the job: the package zip, the PDF, the decks, the `.vtu`, the record, the JSON files. For a laminate: the A, B, D and Q matrices, the mid-plane strains, every ply's stresses, first-ply failure by each criterion and the explanation, in the units picked on the answer.

The live preview (ADR-0086): once the setup has every row it needs, each settled edit is solved by our kernel (`POST /v1/coupon-jobs/live`) and shown under a Preview banner; Run full analysis adds CalculiX, the mesh study and the package (`components/studio/use-live-solve.ts`, `lib/studio-live.ts`).

The model as a file (ADR-0129): once a setup is checked, "Download model" over the model saves it as an "Abaqus input file (.inp)" or an "Ansys input file (.cdb)" (everyone's since ADR-0129 amendment 3), written by the fea package from the rows as they stand (`components/studio/model-download.tsx`, `modelFile` in `lib/studio-api.ts`); the assistant answers a request for the file with the same downloads as buttons (`lib/model-file.ts`, the route `model-file` in `lib/chat-route.ts`).

A model brought in as a file (ADR-0105): where the API offers it (`imports` of `GET /v1/coupon-tests`: where every test is offered, and for a founder's sign-in, ADR-0089 amendment 6), "Import a model" on the left column's head, or beside "Run analysis" on the tension screen, puts the import over the coupon (`components/workspace/import-rail.tsx`, `components/studio/imported-stage.tsx`, `components/studio/use-imported-model.ts`): the Abaqus input file and the units it is written in, then what was read and what was left out, the results in the centre under an "Imported model" banner, and the assistant answering from them (`resultsOnPage`). Nothing in the model is changed, the coupon's rows are not touched, and "Back to the coupon" lets the import go; it is not stored, so a reload lets it go too. A file that is not read is told everything in it that is not, as a list (`importUnreadLines` in `lib/studio-copy.ts`, from the answer's `problem.unread`; ADR-0105 amendment 1), and a moved or turned instance and an edge load are named in what was read.

A past analysis opened again (ADR-0109): `/workspace` lists "Your analyses" under the projects (`components/workspace/chooser.tsx`, `lib/run-history.ts`): the person's finished runs from `GET /v1/coupon-jobs`, each with the answer its run wrote, read by `readJobAnswer` before it is shown. "Open" passes the job's id through the tab's session storage (`askToOpen`, taken once by `ProjectProvider`) and `components/studio/use-past-run.ts` reads the run's status, fields, explanation and rows (`fetchRunValues`). While it is open, `components/workspace/past-rail.tsx` has the left column (the run, its answer, its files, "Use these inputs") and `components/studio/past-stage.tsx` the centre (a "Past run" line and the results view); `resultsOnPage` answers `past` and `readOnly`, so the assistant answers from the run's results and reads no message into a row. Nothing is checked or solved to show it, and the project's rows change only on "Use these inputs". While a past run or an imported model has the stage the chat shows the talk about it alone: the project's own questions, checks, runs and proposals wait behind it and return with the project (ADR-0109 amendment 1). The chat's results card after a run leads with the answer card's verdict and factor, the gauge section's when the coupon has one, and says an earlier failure at a grip edge in one line (`answerStat` in `lib/studio-answer.ts`; ADR-0085 amendment 1).

The answer (ADR-0085): above the summary, a card gives by each criterion whether the coupon fails or holds at the applied loading in the gauge section (the whole model for a coupon without one), R, the loading at first-ply failure, the ply, mode and position, the clamped edge of a grip apart, and the largest ply stresses by sign (`AnswerCard`, `lib/studio-answer.ts`); every number is the package's. The gauge section is read at least a quarter of the coupon's width from each tab line (ADR-0108): the card says the distance, and the page takes the elements read from the fields (`mesh.gauge_elements`).

A question about a result (ADR-0099): the assistant reads "stress in ply 5 at element 120" or "strain here" by fixed patterns (`lib/result-question.ts`) and answers from the fields the page shows, a preview's or a full run's, with the probe table's own cells; "here" is the element clicked on the model, the viewer frames the place, and an element or a ply the model does not hold is refused. No row changes. Where a quantity is largest (ADR-0100) is read the same way and answered with a stored value picked from the gauge section, each ply's extreme under it. With a model on, a loose question the patterns do not read is sent to `POST /v1/workspace/result-questions` with what it may name and no result; the request that comes back is run by the same lookup. Which ply fails first, whether the coupon fails and at what loading (ADR-0099 amendment 1; `lib/result-failure.ts`) is read by patterns of its own, or by the model as the kind `failure`, and answered with the answer card's own values (`answerOf`): three short lines (the ply, the loading with the verdict, labels for what it rests on), with each criterion's row folded under "Details"; "Show on the model" colours that ply's failure index. Every answer about a result has one form (ADR-0099 amendment 2): the answer as the heading, one line of labels for what it is of, a table only for several values, and the rest under "Details" (`valueLines`, `extremeLines`, `failureLines`, `whyLines`). The chat keeps the thread (ADR-0099 amendment 3): a question that names no element is read at the element clicked, else at the gauge centre the package names (`summary.gauge_centre`), and says so; a fragment of a ply, an element or a quantity takes the rest from the question before it (`readFollowUp`); what an element is, and where it sits, is answered from the mesh (`asksElement`, `describeElement`); an answer with Mises, Tresca or a principal stress says they are no composite failure criteria; the failure card shows the applied loading beside the loading at first-ply failure, and a statement of the answer back gets "Yes" only when each number it states is the one the page prints, in its place (`confirmsFailure`).

A question of why (ADR-0102): a message that asks why, or what something means (`lib/result-why.ts`, `asksWhy`), is answered in words when a result is on the page, a preview's or a full run's. The chat sends the question and that result's fact sheet to `POST /v1/workspace/result-answers` and shows the sentences that come back in the page's symbols, with one line under them: the theory sections the answer rests on by document and title, or that the theory pages do not explain this yet. With no answer written it says so and names the sections the question found. With no result on the page it says a question of why is answered from a solved model. No row changes either way.

The working behind an answer (ADR-0103): a message that asks for the formula, the proof, the working or how a value was calculated (`lib/result-working.ts`, `asksWorking`), or the button "Show the working" under a result or an answer of why, shows the chain the fea package wrote in `summary.working` for the criterion named, else the earliest: four steps, each with the values put in, its formula, the formula with the values in place and the theory section it is from. `components/studio/working-steps.tsx` typesets it with KaTeX, loaded when the first working is shown. Nothing is computed in the page and no model is asked. With no result, or for a criterion with no working, the page says so. No row changes.

A message the model reads into nothing (ADR-0088 amendment 1): when the model is on and neither it nor the patterns can enter any of a message, the assistant lists the words it could not use, each with why (not something to set here yet, no value written, or leaning on an earlier message), from the model's `unread` list; the page writes each sentence (`lib/proposals.ts`, `unreadLines`). A card with proposals lists such words under its rows.

The mesh by talking (ADR-0088 amendment 2; `lib/studio-chat.ts`: `readMesh`, `FINER`, `COARSER`): the page's patterns read a written element size and a step from the size the form holds, half for "finer" and twice for "coarser", and the assistant says the size before and after; a question about the mesh changes nothing. It is read in `applyMessage` with the other rows, so with a model on the model still reads first and a step it cannot turn into a value is read by the page. The page sends the size and meshes nothing. At "Who is running it?" a message the patterns read, or one that asks, is not the name.

A plate by talking (ADR-0101): on a plate, with a model on, the assistant reads "hold the bottom edge", "10 kN in tension on the top edge" or, with an element clicked on the checked model or the results, "hold this edge" into supports and loads over the plate's own regions (`lib/proposals.ts`: `plateOffer`, `plateLines`, `plateWith`; `lib/studio-plate.ts`: `regionsHere`). The card lists each to tick; applied, they become rows of Boundary conditions and loads and the setup is checked with them. Under the conversation the page says which regions "here" is. A plate with no support or no load yet shows its shape (amendment 1): the package's mesh and regions with nothing drawn for the missing list (`Preview.shape`, `loaded` null), a line under the model saying what is missing (`shapeWords`), the setup still blocked; a click on it is "here", so the first hold can be "hold this edge". On a plate the live preview does not wait for a tab length or loading rows (`missingIn`), and at "Who is running it?" a plate edit is not taken as the name (`namesPlateEdit`).

Setup words and what is applied (ADR-0110, amendment 1: every program's names for a word): "what is a BC?", "what does encastre mean?" or "what is U2?" is answered from a fixed glossary in our own words (`lib/fea-terms.ts`: `termAsked`, `termLines`), by the page and before the why answers; "what are the boundary conditions?" lists the model's own (`lib/applied.ts`: `appliedOf`); "add a boundary condition", "partition the coupon" or "create a step" (`setupAsked`) says where it is done and changes nothing, except on a plate read by a model, where a boundary condition or load is read into its lists (ADR-0101; its guard fixes the directions of encastre, pinned, XSYMM, YSYMM, ZSYMM, U1 to UR3 and CF1 to CF3, amendment 2). The model draws every boundary condition and load of the preview, before a run and after it (`components/studio/scene-inputs.ts`: `sceneInputs`): a symbol per direction held at a few nodes spread over the set and a load's arrows spread over its set (`lib/studio-geometry.ts`: `sampleNodes`), a plate's proposal dashed until applied (`proposedInputs`), and under the model a list of the boundary conditions, the loads, what is proposed and the model they act on, a line clicked framing its set (`scene-canvas.tsx`: `AppliedList`).

The fibre tow test (ADR-0074): "fibre tow tension" on the Test row swaps the rail's panels for the tow's after the same first step, Test and standard (Project, The fibre tow, Mass per unit length and density, The impregnated strand, The tensile test, Specimens tested, Calculate; `components/workspace/tow-rail.tsx`) and the centre for a sketch of the strand with its gauge length and the result (`components/studio/tow-stage.tsx`). The rows are in the standard's units, the specimens a list of cards (pasted from a spreadsheet if wanted) kept with the project; Calculate sends them to `POST /v1/tow-tests` (`lib/studio-tow.ts`), and the page prints what comes back: each specimen's strength and chord modulus, the mean, s(n-1) and CV, the planning loads, the record judged clause by clause and the report items the record gives.

The wing workspace (ADR-0118): `/workspace/wing`, the third tile of `/workspace`. The left column has the wing file (start from the example, open, save), Geometry, Material, Layup (skin, spar and rib, each with an optional core), Loads (the envelope, the speeds, the gust, the aileron), Aerodynamics, Boundary conditions and Solve (`components/workspace/wing-rail.tsx`); every value is in the file's units. Once the typing settles (350 ms) the description goes to `POST /v1/wings/analyze` (`components/workspace/wing-context.tsx`, `analyzeWing` in `lib/studio-api.ts`) and the centre shows what comes back (`components/workspace/wing-stage.tsx`): the verdict and the lowest margin, the wing from above coloured by its lowest margin along the span, the margins of each check along the span, the box section coloured by shear flow or spanwise load for the case chosen, the design (mass, stiffnesses, first frequency, shear center, how the shear divides, divergence and reversal) and every case. The assistant (`wing-assistant.tsx`) says what governs and what usually raises it, with no number. The wing is kept in this browser (`anisotropy.wing.v1`) and saved and opened as a wing file, which the engine reads; it is not a project yet. Panel 9, Finite element check (ADR-0119), starts `POST /v1/wings/checks` (`startWingFeCheck`), keeps the check's id in this browser (`anisotropy.wing-check.v1`), follows it every 2 s and shows its answer in the centre beside the hand calculation (`FeResults`), marked out of date when the wing has changed since; opening the panel wakes a solver on the hosted tier. The last panel, Shape for CAD, sends the description to `POST /v1/wings/shape`, saves the STEP file (`shapeWing`) and lists each part's thickness, which a CAD program's thicken feature needs.

Drawing code: `lib/studio-geometry.ts` (the plate with four vertices per element face, its deformed shape, the outline frames), `lib/studio-sketch.ts` (the typed rows as a sketch), `lib/studio-colors.ts` (one hue for a magnitude, two hues around a neutral grey for a signed field, fixed slots for ply angles; the scale's ends are the ranges the package writes, ADR-0063), `components/studio/field-material.ts` (the unlit shader that colours the results on the graphics card, with bands and isolines), `components/studio/scene-lighting.tsx` (the lit presentation mode of the model), `lib/studio-fields.ts` (which array a field shows), `lib/studio-form.ts`, `lib/studio-chat.ts`, `lib/laminate-chat.ts` and `lib/studio-api.ts`.

## Out of scope and limits

One test for now (ADR-0089): the page offers the unnotched tension coupon, under ASTM D3039 or no standard, with a laminate of plies, until its comparison with Abaqus is validated. The other tests stay in the code, hidden; `ANISOTROPY_ALL_TESTS=on` on the API shows them for development, and on the hosted tier a founder's sign-in is shown them too (amendment 5); the page asks `GET /v1/coupon-tests`, which answers for the requester. A project or stored coupon of a hidden test is listed as not available yet and is not sent. With the switch off the workspace is the tension screen (ADR-0090): the material and the layup, then Build; the package chooses the rest and the screen lists each choice with its reason. The screen is a model tree (ADR-0111, `lib/model-tree.ts`): Test, Geometry, Material, Layup, Analysis step, Boundary conditions, Loads, Mesh, Solve and Results, each tab with its rows, its checks and the choices made for it ("Chosen for you"); an open Boundary conditions or Loads tab sets `emphasis` in the project, which the model draws, and a change by talking opens its tab (`revealPanel`). Amendment 1 adds a founder's plate (Test offers "Plate" when `GET /v1/coupon-tests` answers `plates`; its Boundary conditions and Loads tabs hold `PlatePanel part="supports"` and `part="loads"`, and `offersAll` sends every row), the Results tab (`components/workspace/results-tab.tsx`: the answer in three lines and buttons that ask the viewer for a field through `showField`, the viewer reporting what it shows through `shownField`), an imported model on the same tabs (`components/workspace/import-rail.tsx`, `importedLines`), and no summary strip above the model on this view. Amendment 2 says a check's warning in its tab (`warnings-<tab>`) with a link from the chat, the second solver's line without counts on this view (`verificationOf(..., brief)`), and the view buttons over the model (`studio-view-tools`: Fit, Top, and the mesh, boundary condition and load layers). The History panel (ADR-0113, `components/workspace/history-tab.tsx`) lists the versions `useVersions` keeps. The panels described above are the development view.

Comparing with Abaqus: a clicked element shows every ply as CAE's Probe Values reads it, by the element's number, at the centroid, with no averaging and in each ply's material axes; CAE averages at nodes by default, so turn averaging off there to compare (ADR-0066 amendment 2).

The user's own Abaqus (ADR-0094, step 6): under the summary of a finished tension run, `components/studio/abaqus-card.tsx` offers the build script and the connector as downloads and takes the connector's file, `<job>-anisotropy.zip`, by drop or chooser. The API stores it for its owner and compares it with this run; the card shows the answer first, then the model, the pull force, the gauge section and between gauge and tabs, each difference with the user's value beside ours and its place, with the full tables under Details. The file can be compared again, downloaded and removed. "The comparison as a PDF" (step 7) asks the API to write the upload's own document and saves it as `<job>-compared-with-anisotropy.pdf`. Where the connector's own route is open (`GET /v1/abaqus-exchange`), the card also creates an upload key, shown once, and gives the command that sends from Abaqus. There, when the run has a build script, the card starts with the quick way (amendment 1): a Send to Abaqus button that hands `anisotropy://open?run=<id>` to the user's PC, and under "First time on this PC" the connector's zip with its setup and Connect this PC, which asks the API for a one-time code and hands it to the PC as `anisotropy://pair?code=...&site=...`; the code is never shown on the page. After Send to Abaqus the card looks for the results for thirty minutes. Under the button it shows one status line at a time, and the three steps by hand fold under "Or do it by hand" (amendment 5); where Send to Abaqus is not offered they stay open. Calls and words are in `lib/abaqus-exchange.ts`; the card computes nothing. It is the one part of the page that names Abaqus (`ABAQUS_CARD_ALLOWS` in `lib/studio-copy.ts`). Tests: `tests/abaqus-exchange.test.ts`, `tests/browser/abaqus-card.spec.ts`.

One job at a time (ADR-0050). Hosted, the site is behind Cloudflare Access on the invite list (ADR-0056); the projects are kept in the browser only, so clearing the site's data loses them and another machine does not see them, until the server store of ADR-0062 part 6. The 3D view needs WebGL 2; without it the page says so and shows the numbers, the checklist, the summary and the explanation. A job's status is lost when the API stops unless run records are on, where the jobs table keeps it (ADR-0056). The colours carry no number: the legend's ticks and the readout do, and the legend's ends are the package's (ADR-0063). The assistants read fixed phrases; a message that names no value is answered with the panel that asks for the next thing, not with a guess. CAD geometry is a placeholder tile.
