# Component card: finite element layer | version 0.3 | updated 2026-10-28

## Purpose and intended use

Read the Abaqus input decks the team builds for coupon and laminate models, solve them with our own laminate membrane kernel on the engine's stiffness, and compare the two number for number (ADR-0029). Built so far: the reader (chunk 1), the membrane kernel with its validation cases (chunk 2), ply stresses in material axes with first-ply failure per element through the engine's criteria (chunk 3), coupon models generated from a parametric spec with a structured mesher and an Abaqus-dialect writer (chunk A1, ADR-0030), CalculiX as the independent solver with a CalculiX-dialect writer, a runner, a `.dat` parser, detected composite conventions and comparison records (chunk B), open-hole coupons on a Gmsh transfinite O-grid with the Kirsch validation case and a CalculiX record (chunk A2), the coupon input form and its values file (ADR-0046), one command from the input to the package (ADR-0049), and the studio's foundations (ADR-0051): the setup checklist (`checks.py`, from the tools' own refusals, the mesh quality measures of `quality.py` and what is installed), the preview of a model (`preview.py`), every field of a solve as JSON with its summary (`fields.py`) and the template explanation (`explain.py`), answered through `fea/tools/studio_tool.py`), and plate bending (ADR-0053): an MITC4 flat shell with five degrees of freedom per node on the laminate's A, B, D and transverse shear stiffness (`shell.py`), dispatched by `kernel.solve_deck` for decks that bend, carry a pressure, load or prescribe a degree of freedom out of the plane or have an unsymmetric layup; `*Dload` pressures; both faces of every ply in the recovery; three- and four-point bending coupons from the spec and the form; five plate and strip validation cases against closed forms and the first bending coupon against CalculiX S8R. Shear coupons and the eight-node element follow.

## Out of scope / do not use for

Anything Abaqus can do that the reader refuses (see the table in [fea/README.md](../../fea/README.md)): three-dimensional elements, triangles, rotated laminate axes, translated instances, nonlinear or multi-step analyses, distributed loads other than a uniform pressure per element, loads or moments on the drilling rotation (dof 6). Buckling, thermal strain, interlaminar stresses and progressive failure are not computed; the shell's transverse shear stress is uniform through the thickness and not reported per ply; force control of a bending nose is not built; a CalculiX deck with a pressure is not written yet. Maximum strain is refused because a deck carries no strain limits; Tsai-Wu needs the deck's `*Fail Stress` interaction term. Abaqus-style failure indices (MSTRS, TSAIH, TSAIW) are not produced yet. Abaqus `.odb` files are read only through the extraction script under `abaqus python` (ADR-0045); CAE field-report CSV files are read (ADR-0030 amendment 2); `.rpt` files are not. The mesh quality thresholds of `quality.py` are our own and only warn. The kernel's full-integration bilinear element is not Abaqus's reduced-integration S4R; the two agree for uniform fields and differ at a level to be measured in the first comparison note (risk R-15).

## Inputs / outputs

Input: an Abaqus `.inp` file and the unit system it is written in (`mm-MPa` or `m-Pa`), stated by the caller. Output: a `Deck` in SI base units with parts, elements, node and element sets, composite shell sections (plies bottom-first, the `symmetric` flag applied), materials with `*Elastic` and `*Fail Stress`, instances, reference nodes, kinematic couplings, boundary conditions and the one static step; the skipped output keywords; the file's SHA-256; notes. `section_plies` turns a section into the engine's `Ply` list, and `is_symmetric_layup` says whether `B` vanishes. Unsupported content raises `DeckError` with the line number (ADR-0003). `kernel.solve_deck(deck)` returns a `MembraneSolution` (nodal displacements, reactions at prescribed dofs, the reference node's reaction being the sum over its coupled region, membrane strains at element centroids and Gauss points, section forces `N = A eps`) or, for a deck that bends, a `ShellSolution` (five dofs per node, curvatures, transverse shear strains, moments and shear forces too), all in SI; `recovery.element_ply_states` gives each element's ply strains and stresses in laminate and material axes, on both faces of every ply under bending; `recovery.strength_ratios(solution, criterion, f12_star)` gives every ply's strength ratio and the first-ply-failure factor with its element, ply, face and mode; `cases.py` compares a solve with expected numbers in the deck's units, including per-ply (and per-face) stresses, deflections, rotations, moments, shear forces and the failure factor. Theory: [fea/docs/theory.md](../../fea/docs/theory.md).

## Dependencies and versions

Python 3.12+, `anisotropy-engine` (editable path dependency), NumPy, SciPy (for the kernel in the next chunk), Pydantic 2 (`fea/uv.lock`). No Abaqus software is needed to read a deck.

## Known limitations

The Abaqus sign convention for compressive limits in `*Fail Stress` and the layer order semantics of `symmetric` are UNVERIFIED against Abaqus documentation; the reader stores values as written and consumers use magnitudes and bottom-first order (ADR-0004). Set and material names must match exactly as written (Abaqus itself is case-insensitive). Units are stated, not inferred; a wrong statement is caught only by the modulus plausibility warning.

## Failure modes seen

- 2026-10-15: the team's deck defines `*Nset, nset=s_Set-4` twice, once with `instance=` and once naming the assembly reference node of that very coupling. The reader unions the two and drops the reference node from the coupled nodes with a note.
