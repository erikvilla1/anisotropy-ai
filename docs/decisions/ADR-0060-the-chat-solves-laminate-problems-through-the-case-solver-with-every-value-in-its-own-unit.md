## Context

The studio's guided chat (ADR-0055) sets up a coupon test: every question leads to a finite element job. A student or engineer with a hand-calculation problem (a stack, its material constants, the loads per unit width; the A, B, D matrices, the mid-plane strains, every ply's stresses and first-ply failure) had no way in: the chat read only coupon phrases, the home page calculator (ADR-0059) takes a library material and loads in SI base units, and the case endpoint `POST /v1/cases/solve` (ADR-0024) wants the case format, with one unit system for the whole problem. Textbook problems mix units: E1 in GPa, E2 in MPa, the strengths in MPa, the ply thickness in mm and the loads in N/mm, or Msi, ksi, inches and lb/in. The site's inputs were labelled GPa and mm with no choice, and the engine's `UNIT_SYSTEMS` had SI (GPa, MPa, mm, N/m), US (Msi, ksi, in, lb/in) and psi, but no system in N/mm and MPa, the one most hand calculations are done in.

The engine computes every number (ADR-0001), works in SI inside with conversion only at entry and exit (ADR-0002), and refuses what it cannot read rather than assuming (ADR-0003).

## Options considered

1. Convert in the browser: a unit menu next to each input, the page multiplies by the factor and sends SI. Rejected: a conversion is a number the page would compute (ADR-0001), the factor table would exist twice (TypeScript and Python) and could drift, and the run record (ADR-0026) would show SI values the engineer never typed.
2. Extend the case format with tagged values (`E1 = {value = 147, unit = "GPa"}`) and send the chat's problem through `POST /v1/cases/solve`. Rejected: the case format is the validation suite's format, read by every case under `engine/validation/cases/` and by the founder's own case files; a second way of writing a value there changes every reader, and the chat would still need the summary, the failure analysis and the prose that the case endpoint does not return.
3. A separate summary endpoint beside the case endpoint, called after it. Rejected as in ADR-0059: two requests and two run records per problem.
4. One endpoint for the chat's problem, `POST /v1/laminates/solve`: every value carries its own unit; the API converts each with the engine's unit table into a case in the reporting system the engineer asked for, runs the case solver (`validate_case_inputs`, `solve_case`, `engine_results`, `first_ply_failures`, `laminate_summary`), and returns the results in that system with the labels, the facts the explanation is written from, and the template's or the model's sentences under the number guard (ADR-0052). The unit table and the four systems are one table in `units.py`, exported to `data/units.json`, which the site imports for its menus, so the page lists only units the engine converts and converts nothing itself. Chosen.

For the reporting system: a fourth system `mm-MPa` (MPa, mm, N/mm, N·mm/mm, N/mm for A, N for B, N·mm for D) joins SI, US and psi, and the result page's toggle re-solves the problem in the system picked rather than converting the answer. For the chat's reading of typed text: fixed patterns, as ADR-0055 decided against an LLM reading messages; a value typed without a unit takes the system's unit, a value typed with a unit of another dimension is refused by name, and the six loads must all be stated (a button fills the rest with zero, which is then a stated zero). For the layup convention: the laminate problem counts angles from x, the direction of Nx, as the textbooks do; the coupon chat keeps ADR-0004's convention across the width, and both questions say so.

## Decision

Option 4. `units.py` gains `UNITS` (Pa, kPa, MPa, GPa, psi, ksi, Msi; m, cm, mm, in; N/m, N/mm, kN/m, kN/mm, lb/in, kip/in; N·m/m, N·mm/mm, kN·m/m, lb·in/in, kip·in/in), `QUANTITY_DIMENSIONS`, `units_of`, `to_si`, `from_si` and `in_system`, the `mm-MPa` system, and `data/units.json` held equal to the table by a test. The API's `LaminateProblem` takes a library material or the engineer's own constants as `{value, unit}` quantities (all five strengths or none), the layup bottom ply first, the ply thickness, the six loads, an optional f12* and `report_units`; `LaminateAnswer` carries the case the engine solved, every result of `engine_results` in the reporting units with their labels, the criteria evaluated, the summary and the material's provenance, the facts and the prose with its writer. The chat's first choice, "Laminate response", opens the laminate assistant (ADR-0055 amendment 2): units, material, layup, loads, review, solve; the answer beside the conversation with the unit toggle, the matrices, every ply's stresses in fibre or laminate axes, the first-ply failure table and the explanation.

## Consequences

Easier: a hand-calculation problem is solved in the chat in the units it is stated in, with every number the engine's and the conversion in one table; the unit list the page shows cannot differ from what the engine accepts. Harder: a new unit means a line in `units.py` and `units.json` regenerated, and a new reporting system means a line in `UNIT_SYSTEMS` and a title in `REPORT_SYSTEMS`; the chat's readers are fixed patterns, so a phrasing they do not know is answered with the cards, not guessed. Not done here: the coupon flow (the full form, the decks, the PDF) is still in mm and MPa; its unit choice is a separate change, since the specs, deck headers and documents print those units.

## How we will know it was wrong

- Engineers type values the readers do not catch and give up before the cards; or a refused unit stops them where a conversion would have been right.
- The mm-MPa labels do not match what a course or a customer writes (N·mm/mm for a moment per unit width, say), and the answer is misread.

## Amendment 1, 2026-11-06 (maximum strain by limits derived from the strengths)

**Context.** The chat's first choice promises first-ply failure, and the engine evaluates four criteria (ADR-0021), but the laminate problem never stated the maximum-strain choice: `case_of` wrote the strengths only, so the case never carried `strain_limits`, the engine evaluated three criteria, and the results panel said nothing about the fourth. The founder asked for the flow to be checked end to end before engineers use it (2026-11-05: "make sure that we are healthy and good to go ... ply failure etc").

**Options.** (1) Evaluate maximum strain always, with the limits derived from the strengths: rejected, a derived limit assumes linear behaviour to failure, and ADR-0021 makes it a stated choice. (2) Five typed strain limits: a measured limit is rare in a homework problem and the engine's explicit keys stay reachable through the case endpoint. (3) The choice on the material, as the case format states it, from a checkbox on the review card: chosen.

**Decision.** `OwnMaterial` and `LibraryChoice` take `strain_limits: "from-strengths" | None`; typed constants without the five strengths are refused by name; `case_of` copies the key through and the engine derives the limits. The chat's draft carries the choice as a boolean the review card's checkbox sets, `buildProblem` sends it with the material and refuses it without five strengths, and the results panel names the criterion "(limits from the strengths)" and says when maximum strain was not computed. The explanation's withheld reason gets its own line, `explanation-reason`, as the coupon results have.

**Evidence.** `cd api && uv run pytest -q tests/test_laminate_problems.py`: the homework problem with the choice gives the four criteria and `fpf_ratio_max_strain` equal to the SI case with the same key to 1e-12, the library material likewise, the refusals by name; `npm test` (`tests/laminate-chat.test.ts`); `tests/browser/studio.spec.ts` ticks the box, sends `strain_limits` with the material and reads R = 3.22 by maximum strain beside R = 3.207 by Tsai-Wu, from fixtures captured anew from the API. The live model: the laminate answer in SI and in US units written by `claude-opus-5-5` on 2026-11-06, both passing the guard, recorded in [the assistant evaluation](../evaluations/api/2026-10-28-assistant.md); the counts and outputs are in the pull request.
