## Context

The membrane kernel of ADR-0029 solves flat, symmetric-layup coupons in plane stress: two degrees of freedom per node, `B = 0` required, no out-of-plane load. The spec of ADR-0030 already validates the three- and four-point bending kinds but refused to build them; the studio plan (ADR-0051) asked for a bending element so that bending coupons, pressures on plates and unsymmetric layups can be solved by our engine with CalculiX as the reference, which the founder confirmed on 2026-10-28. The team's models use Abaqus S4R shells with six degrees of freedom per node and `*Dload` pressures; CalculiX's composite shell is S8R, expanded to a brick. The laminate law with `B` and `D` is already in the engine (ADR-0004, ADR-0013); the engine's ply recovery takes a curvature.

## Options considered

1. **MITC4 Reissner-Mindlin flat shell, four nodes, five degrees of freedom (u, v, w, theta_x, theta_y), transverse shear interpolated from the edge midpoints (Bathe and Dvorkin), 2 by 2 Gauss integration, the laminate law N = A eps_0 + B kappa, M = B eps_0 + D kappa, Q = H gamma with the shear correction 5/6.** Chosen. It keeps the existing corner-node meshes, decks and couplings; it does not lock on thin plates; it carries transverse shear as S4R and S8R do, so a thick coupon compares; the drilling rotation (dof 6) is dropped, which a flat plate allows.
2. **Discrete Kirchhoff quadrilateral (DKQ).** No transverse shear deformation: a thick coupon or a short span could not match S8R; its stiffness derivation is longer. Rejected.
3. **Eight-node reduced-integration shell (the kernel's own S8R).** Midside nodes in every mesh, writer and set; spurious modes with reduced integration; a larger change for the same comparisons. Rejected for now (the eight-node element remains a later chunk for the gradient regions, ADR-0030's chunk E).
4. **A displacement-based bilinear plate (the same element without the tying).** Locks as the plate gets thin: on a plate a thousand times thinner than wide it gives a deflection orders of magnitude too small. Rejected; the thin-plate case pins the difference.

Conventions decided with the element, each a choice the record states: rotations about the axes as Abaqus numbers them, so u = u_0 + z theta_y and v = v_0 - z theta_x (Whitney's psi_x = theta_y, psi_y = -theta_x); the shear correction factor k = 5/6 for every laminate alike (Reissner's value; Whitney, Section 10.4, lists 5/6, pi^2/12 and 2/3 as the values in use); a pressure written with the Abaqus P label, positive against the element normal; the dispatch `kernel.solve_deck` keeps the membrane kernel for every deck it can solve and sends to the shell only a deck that bends, carries a pressure, prescribes or loads a degree of freedom out of the plane, or has an unsymmetric layup, so every in-plane result the committed cases and records pin stays the one they pin; a bending coupon rests on two support lines where w is held, is moved by its nose line(s), and is pinned in its plane at the centre node of each support line (both directions at the left support, the across direction at the right); the ply recovery keeps both faces of every ply under bending and names the failing face.

## Decision

Build the flat shell of option 1 in `fea/src/anisotropy_fea/shell.py` with the conventions above; dispatch through `kernel.solve_deck`; read `*Dload` with the P label and boundary conditions and loads on dofs 3 to 5; build the three- and four-point bending coupons from the spec with supports, pins and noses; keep both faces of every ply in the recovery; compare with CalculiX S8R through records that hold RF_z, w and the faces; put the bending tests on the input form (form 0.4, ADR-0046 amendment 4) and in the studio.

## Why (evidence)

- Pure bending engine equality: `fea/checks/cases/bend-1x1-im7-engine-equality.toml`, 64 numbers at 1.8e-7 of the 1e-9 tolerance (`tests/test_fe_validation_cases.py`, `tests/test_shell_solve.py`).
- Closed forms: `uv run python fea/tools/plate_closed_forms.py --converge` on 2026-10-28: the isotropic plate's centre deflection -0.51, -0.13 and -0.03 % at 8, 16 and 32 elements a side and its centre moment -1.07, -0.26 and -0.06 %; the thin plate (h/a = 1e-3) the same numbers to three digits; the cross-ply plate -3.7, -0.94 and -0.23 % on the deflection; the strip's tip deflection -0.016 %, its tip rotation, root moment and shear force to 1e-9. The element matrix is symmetric with exactly six zero eigenvalues on a rectangle and on a skewed quadrilateral (`tests/test_shell_element.py`).
- The membrane reproduced: the shell on the one-element symmetric coupon with w and the tilts held gives the membrane's u, v, strain, N and reactions to 1e-12, and through the gripped coupon's couplings to 1e-10 (`tests/test_shell_solve.py`).
- CalculiX: `fea/checks/three-point-im7-8552/records/three-point-im7-8552-calculix.toml` and its reading; the detection run of the pressure sign in `fea/checks/calculix-conventions.toml` (repeated by `tests/test_calculix.py` whenever CalculiX is present).
- Sources: J. M. Whitney, *Structural Analysis of Laminated Anisotropic Plates* (1987), Chapter 5 (the Navier solution, Eqs. 5.1 to 5.10, 5.16) and Chapter 10 (Eqs. 10.1 to 10.7, 10.19, 10.20, 10.25; the k values of Section 10.4), read from the copy under `.local/sources/`; K.-J. - The Abaqus convention for the P label (positive against the normal) is taken from the kernel's own statement and not verified against the Abaqus documentation: UNVERIFIED. A comparison with Abaqus on a bending coupon: no evidence yet.

## Consequences

- Easier: bending coupons, pressures on plates and unsymmetric layups solve in our engine with the same meshes, decks, records and studio; the plate kind (ADR-0054) can add supports and loads on any region.
- Harder: two kernels to keep equal where they overlap (pinned by `test_shell_solve.py`); a record of a bending coupon holds faces, so the document and the audit rows that read one value per ply need the face-aware readers before a bending coupon is audited; the drilling rotation is dropped, so a deck that loads it is refused.
- We now depend on the MITC4 tying for thin plates (risk R-21) and on CalculiX's shell conventions for bending (the pressure sign and the set totals, recorded).

## How we will know it was wrong

- A validation case of this record fails after a change, or a refined mesh of a plate case moves away from its closed form instead of toward it (the convergence table).
- A bending coupon's CalculiX record, or a team's Abaqus S4R result, disagrees with the kernel beyond the record's tolerance on the deflection or the reaction, and the cause is not an element difference under a line load.
- A coupon thinner than the thin-plate case locks, or a skewed mesh fails the six-rigid-mode check.
