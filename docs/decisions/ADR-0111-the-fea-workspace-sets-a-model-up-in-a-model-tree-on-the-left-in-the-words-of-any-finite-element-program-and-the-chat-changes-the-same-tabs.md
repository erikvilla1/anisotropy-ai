## Context

- The product's view of the FEA workspace (the switch of ADR-0089 off: customers and founders) was the tension screen of ADR-0090: "Material and layup" with two choices, a "Change settings" panel holding the size, the loading and the mesh, "Model settings" listing what the software chose, and "Files". Boundary conditions, the analysis step and the mesh had no place of their own; the founder could not find where a setting lives.
- Engineers set a model up in a finite element pre-processor: a tree on the left, one branch per part of the analysis, in the order it is built (geometry, material and section, step, boundary conditions, loads, mesh, job, results). The founder asked for that, with the assistant changing the same settings, and for it to be no one program's: models written by Abaqus come in today (ADR-0105) and Ansys models are to come.
- ADR-0090 chose the short screen on 2026-11-11 ("Two choices + chat"). Its rule stands: the screen sends only the rows the engineer may change, and the fea package chooses every other row and says why. A coupon's grips are not a row of the screen: the blank form's grip kind is the Excel form's ("coupling"), and the package chooses clamped grips ("nodal") for the screen.
- The glossary of ADR-0110 knew one program's names only (`lib/abaqus-terms.ts`).

## Options considered

1. **A model tree on the left in general FEA words, ten tabs from the test to the results, each holding its rows, the checklist items that judge them and the software's choices for it; the chat changes the same rows and opens the tab it changed (chosen).** The engineer sees the whole setup in the order of any pre-processor, and what the chat does lands where it can be seen. The tree is longer than the two-choice screen, so the tabs that need nothing start closed with their values in one line.
2. **Keep the screen of ADR-0090 and rename its panels.** Rejected: boundary conditions, the step and the mesh would still have no place, which is what the founder could not find.
3. **The development view's panels (ADR-0062) for everyone.** Rejected: about ninety rows, most of them with one right answer for a D3039 coupon, the reason ADR-0090 was made.
4. **A tree in one program's words and layout (its module names, its icons).** Rejected by the founder ("not an abaqus style panel"): Ansys models are to come in, and the product names no program (ADR-0091).

## Decision

- **The tabs.** Test, Geometry, Material, Layup, Analysis step, Boundary conditions, Loads, Mesh, Solve, Results, numbered in that order (`MODEL_TREE`). Each tab holds the rows of the screen it edits, once; the checklist items that judge them give its badge; the rows the package chose for it are listed in it under "Chosen for you" with their reason. A closed tab says its values in one line ("250 × 25 mm, 56 mm tabs", "1 mm pull", "4 mm elements, 300 in all"). Run analysis heads the tree. The rows sent are those of ADR-0090, unchanged.
- **What a coupon's tabs hold.** Analysis step: one static linear step, said, not changed. Boundary conditions: the coupon's two, in the words under the model ("Bottom grip: fixed (encastre)"), set by its test and changed by no row. Loads: the load in the same words, then the loading type and value, a blank row saying what stands for it ("displacement, chosen for you").
- **The tree and the model.** While Boundary conditions or Loads is open, the model draws that part's sets with heavier frames, before and after a run.
- **The tree and the chat.** A change made by talking, by the page's patterns or a model's proposal, opens the tab that holds the row and marks it for a moment. The chat's links to fix an item or to edit open the tab, by the same lookup.
- **No program's words.** The tab names are general FEA words; the glossary (ADR-0110 amendment 1) answers each program's name for a setup word with one answer.
- **Not in this record.** Boundary conditions on any edge or face of a plate (ADR-0101) on the product's view is the founder's choice, asked on 2026-11-17 and not yet answered; until then the plate stays where ADR-0089 amendment 6 put it.

## Consequences

- The setup of the product's view reads like any pre-processor, and a change by talking shows where it went.
- The tree is longer than the two-choice screen; the closed tabs keep it to one line each.
- A new kind of model (a plate, an imported model) needs its own tabs' contents; the tab names stay.

## How we will know it was wrong

- The founder or an engineer still asks where a setting is, or opens the wrong tab for it.
- A change made by talking does not open the tab that holds it, or opens another.
- Engineers coming from another program do not recognise a tab's name.

## Amendment 1, 2026-11-18 (a plate's boundary conditions and loads on any edge or face, a Results tab, an imported model on the same tree, and no summary above the model; asked for by the founder)

**Context.** Asked on 2026-11-17 what more the tree needs, the answer offered four things: boundary conditions and loads on any edge or face, an imported model on the same tree, a Results tab that does more than list the files, and no summary strip above the model now that the tree says the setup. The founder answered "yes add them the first one sounds very important because it enables the user to interact with the model".

**Options.**
1. *All four on the same tree and the same pull request (chosen).*
2. The plate alone first. Rejected: the founder asked for all four.
3. A separate screen for the plate, as the development view has (ADR-0101). Rejected: the tree's own Boundary conditions and Loads tabs are where the founder looks for them.

**Decision.**
- **A plate on the tree.** Where it is offered ([ADR-0089](ADR-0089-only-unnotched-tension-under-d3039-or-no-standard-is-offered-until-its-abaqus-comparison-is-validated.md) amendment 7: a founder's sign-in, proposed), Test offers "Tension coupon" or "Plate". A plate's Geometry tab holds its size and its regions; Boundary conditions and Loads each hold their list (`PlatePanel part=…`), say it in one line ("1 on Edge-Left") and are marked missing while empty. A support's directions are ticked as x, y, z, θx, θy and θz, in no program's notation; the development view keeps U1 to UR3 (ADR-0110). Held or loaded by a message, a proposal card or a click on the model, the tab that changed opens and is marked. The line above the tree says what is still needed ("a boundary condition, a load"). A plate sends every row; a tension coupon still sends ADR-0090's rows only.
- **Results.** The Results tab says the answer in three lines (reaction, largest displacement, first-ply failure and its R), offers buttons to colour the model by the failure index, the displacement or a ply's stress or strain, and a ply to show; the button of the field shown is pressed, whether it was chosen here or in the viewer. The report and files follow once the run has ended. Every number is the result's own.
- **An imported model.** Its left column is the same tree after "File and units": Geometry, Material, Layup, Analysis step, Boundary conditions, Loads, Mesh, Solve, Results and "Left out", each line the reader's own report (`importedLines`), no row to change. Its Boundary conditions and Loads list what is drawn and light it while open; its Results tab is the coupon's, with no files.
- **No summary above the model.** On the product's view the strip that repeated the setup above the model is gone; the development view keeps it.

**Evidence.** `npm test`: 422 tests, 421 pass, 1 skipped. `npm run typecheck`: clean. `cd api && uv run pytest -q tests/test_coupon_tests.py`: 20 passed, 1 skipped. `cd fea && uv run pytest -q tests/test_coupon_document.py`: 25 passed, 1 skipped. The whole studio browser suite: 53 of 55 passed; the two that failed (the chat's run note said "model", and the audit read the strip that is gone) were fixed, the note now "Progress is shown in the centre of the page" and the audit reading the Loads tab's "1 mm pull", and passed with the tree's, the plate's and the glossary's scenarios on a fresh build (8 passed). `tests/browser/expansion.spec.ts` and `portfolio.spec.ts`, not run by CI, read the tree in place of the strip (2 passed). `tests/model-tree.test.ts`: an imported model's tab titles and one-line values from the reader's report; what a plate still needs; a list in one line. ## Amendment 2, 2026-11-18 (a tab says its warnings, the second solver's check without its counts, and the model's own view buttons; chosen by the founder)

**Context.** The founder shared a review of the workspace on 2026-11-17 and, of the gaps offered from it, chose three: "point 2 3 and 4 is good". Point 2: a warning that strengths are missing should name them and lead to where they are given. Point 3: the model view should fit and reset its camera and show or hide the mesh, the boundary conditions and the loads. Point 4 is [ADR-0113](ADR-0113-each-settled-change-to-a-setup-is-kept-as-a-version-in-the-browser-with-who-made-it-why-and-its-runs-answer-and-going-back-is-a-new-version.md). Point 1, an open comparison with the second solver, he turned down: "the 57 of 57 agree makes no sense that is something the user shouldn't be trying to verify".

**Options.**
1. *The three he chose, and the second solver's line without counts on the product's view (chosen).*
2. Keep "Verified against reference solver: 57 of 57 values agree" for customers. Rejected by the founder's words above.
3. Remove the line for customers. Not chosen: whether a second solver agrees is the one thing of it a customer reads; the founder was told the line would read "Checked by a second solver" and may still ask for it to go.

**Decision.**
- **Warnings in their tab.** A check's warning is said in the tab whose items it judges (`warnings-<tab>`), and the chat's warning carries a link to that tab ("Open Material"). The fea package's warning for a material without strengths names the five it needs: F1t, F1c, F2t, F2c and F6.
- **The second solver.** On the product's view the run's line reads "Checked by a second solver", or "A second solver differs on some values: treat this result with care"; the development view keeps the counts.
- **The view.** Over the model: Fit (the whole model, as first shown), Top (straight above), and Mesh, Boundary conditions and Loads, each shown or hidden. A model of another size or place is framed again on its own; a finer mesh of the same plate is not.

**Evidence.** `tests/studio-copy.test.ts`: the brief line, agreeing and not. Browser, "the model tree": the Material tab's warning names the five strengths and the chat's "Open Material" opens it; the line reads "Checked by a second solver"; a layer hidden and shown and the view moved to Top and back. `cd fea && uv run pytest -q tests/test_checks.py tests/test_values_input.py`: 52 passed, 1 failed, the check that names Gmsh when it is missing, because this machine has no Gmsh (an environment failure, the same on main). The whole studio browser suite: 55 passed. UNVERIFIED: not deployed.

## Amendment 3, 2026-11-21 (the tree opens from an icon rail as a flyout; chosen by the founder)

**Context.** In layout D, which the founder picked on 2026-11-21 ([ADR-0134](ADR-0134-the-fea-workspace-is-the-model-across-the-window-with-the-chat-full-height-on-the-right-and-a-results-card-under-the-model.md)), the model takes the window and the tree folds to an icon rail on its left; the mockup showed the results card in two columns while the tree is open.

**Options.** (1) The tree as a fixed column, as before: rejected, it takes the room the model was given. (2) The tree as a flyout from an icon rail, one button per tab, the same tabs, rows and words: chosen. (3) One tab at a time in the flyout: not chosen, the engineer reads the setup down the tree as a program shows it, and the tabs' lines say each value.

**Decision.** The icon rail holds a button for each tab (Test, Geometry, Material, Layup, Analysis step, Boundary conditions, Loads, Mesh, Solve, Results, History, in the tree's order) and one for the whole tree. A button opens the flyout beside the rail with that tab open and in view; pressed again with its tab in view, it closes the flyout. The flyout holds the tree as before, Run analysis at its top, and the model's area gives it the room. It is open while a project is set up and closes once when a run's results first show; the engineer's own opening or closing stands after that. T opens and closes it, Esc closes it. A tab the assistant changes opens the flyout, and the tab is marked as before. Boundary conditions and Loads still light their part of the model while open.

**Evidence.** The founder's choice of layout D; no evidence yet that the flyout is found as easily as the column.

## Amendment 4, 2026-11-24 (the chosen material's numbers under the choice; asked for by the founder)

**Context.** The Material tab showed only the select: a library material's constants appeared nowhere on the tree, though the mockup of layout D lists them under the choice. The founder, 2026-11-24: "i know i selected the material but I want the material numbers to show up like you have in the workspace canvas demo you showed".

**Options.** (1) The numbers from the setup check's preview (`preview.material`): rejected, they show only after a check, and a check's preview can be for an earlier choice. (2) The numbers the engine already writes into the form's definition, in every unit system: chosen; they show the moment the material is picked. (3) The page converting the library's MPa values itself: rejected, the page converts nothing (ADR-0061).

**Decision.** Under the select, for a library material, the tab lists "From the library" and two columns as the mockup does: E1, E2 and G12 in GPa or Msi, ν12, and the ply thickness when the library gives one, on the left; F1t, F1c, F2t, F2c and F6 in the coupon's stress unit on the right, or a line saying the library gives no strengths. `fea/tools/export_form_definition.py` writes them under `library_values`, converted by `anisotropy_fea.units`; the page prints them by the one number rule (four significant figures, so 2558.5 MPa reads 2559 MPa). The engineer's own material shows nothing more: its numbers are the fields they typed. The failure criteria stay in the tab's "Chosen for you" table.

**Evidence.** `tests/tension-screen.test.ts` (IM7/8552 in mm-MPa and in-psi, a record without ply thickness, one without strengths); `tests/browser/studio.spec.ts`, the tension screen's test, reads the ten values the moment IM7/8552 is picked; `fea/tests/test_studio_tool.py` fails if the definition drifts from the package.

## Amendment 5, 2026-11-24 (G13 and G23 in the Material step, the engineer's own optional; asked for by the founder)

**Context.** The library gives no G13 or G23. The tension coupon has always written estimates for them when blank (`tension_input`: G13 = G12; G23 = E2 / 3 for a tape, G12 for a fabric), listed after a check under "Chosen for you", and a library material had no place to give measured values: the screen dropped them. They cannot be left out of the model: the shell's transverse shear, CalculiX's brick and the Abaqus and Ansys files all need them, though a pull on a symmetric layup hardly loads them. The founder, 2026-11-24, on the material numbers of amendment 4: "where can i put G13 and G23, also to give me the option of including it or not".

**Options.** (1) "Not included" meaning the model has no G13 and G23: rejected, the model cannot be built without them. (2) Show the estimates, marked, with a box to give one's own: chosen; unticked, the estimates are used as before; ticked, two fields take the place of the two rows, a blank field keeping its estimate. (3) Always show the two fields: rejected, most engineers have no measured value and the block would ask for what it does not need.

**Decision.** The stiffness column lists G13 and G23 after G12, marked "est.", with a line giving the rule. "Use my own G13 and G23" shows two fields in the coupon's stress unit, the estimates as their placeholders; what is typed is sent with the library material and kept by the package, which no longer estimates that row. Unticking empties the fields; picking another material starts from its estimates. The estimates come from one function, `tension_input.transverse_estimates`, which builds the coupon and writes them into the form's definition, so what the page shows is what the model gets. The setup check's note on a G13 or G23 far from G12 is unchanged.

**Evidence.** `fea/tests/test_tension_input.py` (the estimates shown are the ones the coupon is built with; given values are kept; one given, the other estimated); `tests/tension-screen.test.ts` (the rows, the placeholders, a fabric's rule, what is sent); `tests/browser/studio.spec.ts` (ticking, typing 5200 and 3400 MPa, and the check receiving them; unticking; a new material).

## Amendment 6, 2026-11-25 (every constant of a library material is the engineer's to change; asked for by the founder)

**Context.** Amendment 4 put the chosen material's constants under the choice and amendment 5 made G13 and G23 the engineer's own when they have measured them. Every other constant stayed read-only: to change E1 the engineer had to pick "My own material" and type all fourteen rows again. The founder's team, who work in Abaqus and Ansys, asked for the values to be visible and editable; the founder, 2026-11-25: "well even through we select the material and automatically populates the material properties, we should stil be able to edit". One of their own models carries G13 = 140,929 MPa and G23 = 9,722 MPa for IM7/8552 against our estimates of 4,688 and 2,988, which is the kind of difference an engineer has to be able to settle in the page.

**Options.** (1) The typed values ride along with the library pick, as G13, G23 and the ply thickness already do, and the record stays cited: rejected, the spec's `[material]` table is either a library record or a set of constants, so a third shape would have to be carried through `spec.py`, the Abaqus, Ansys and CalculiX writers and the report, and the coupon would go on citing a record whose numbers it no longer uses. (2) Editing copies the record into the engineer's own rows, which the spec already holds: chosen. (3) Leave it, and let the engineer retype the material: rejected, that is the complaint.

**Decision.** Under the material's values, "Edit these values" copies the chosen record into the own-material rows and clears the library id (`ownFromLibrary`, `lib/tension-screen.ts`). Every constant goes across exactly as the engine wrote it, in the row's own unit, from `library_constants` and `library_values` in the form's definition, so copying and changing nothing changes no number; the page converts nothing (ADR-0001, ADR-0002). G13 and G23 carry the estimates the coupon was already built with, or the engineer's own where they gave them. The name is the record's id in capitals (`IM7-8552`, and `M7781-5245C` where an id starts with a digit) and the source names the record and says the values were edited here. The record is no longer cited, because the numbers are no longer only its own; the coupon's material code therefore becomes a free `M<n>` rather than the record's, by the existing rule that a code means one thing (ADR-0038 amendment 1). Picking a material from the list again puts the library's own numbers back.

**Evidence.** `tests/tension-screen.test.ts` ("editing a library material copies its record into the engineer's own rows, changing no number": every constant against `libraryConstantsIn` and `libraryValuesIn`, the estimates, the name of an id that starts with a digit, and what `tensionValues` sends). Solved both ways with `fea/tools/studio_tool.py live` on the IM7/8552 \[45/0/-45/90]s coupon: every number in `summary.json` agrees to about 1 part in 10^10 (reaction 16211.230873790262 N against 16211.230873932433 N), the two paths differing only in the order they convert units. The one visible difference is which of the two equal and opposite ±45° plies is named for peak S12 (45.77158881848687 MPa in ply 3 against −45.77158882773952 MPa in ply 1, the same element): a tie the round-off breaks either way, which predates this change.

## Amendment 7, 2026-11-25 (the Results tab shows the stress of the whole laminate and the deformation along each direction, its fields in groups; drafted, not yet confirmed by the founder)

**Context.** The founder passed on one more point from his team, who work in Abaqus and Ansys (2026-11-25, "an extra thing that my teammate mentioned is", with their note): the Results tab seemed to show ply stresses only; there should be a way to see the stress of the whole composite, and the total deformation and the deformation in a direction. The tab offered nine fields in one row of buttons: the failure index, the displacement's size, the deflection where the model bends, and each ply's three stresses and three strains. The package already wrote the displacement along x and y, and the viewer's own menu listed them, but the tab did not. Nothing the package wrote was a stress of the laminate as a whole: the nearest was the section force, a force per unit width.

**Options.** For the stress of the whole composite: (1) the laminate's mean stress through its thickness, the section force over the thickness, in the model's axes: chosen; it is what a test reports as load over area, it needs no ply chosen, and it is one more field of the same kind as the section force. (2) An envelope, the largest of a ply stress over every ply in one picture: not built; it puts plies of different angles, each in its own fibre axes, on one scale, and the picture that says where the laminate is worst is already there as the failure index of the worst ply. It stays open if the team meant this. (3) A von Mises stress of the laminate: rejected, it is not a composite's measure (ADR-0066 amendment 2 keeps Mises for comparison only). (4) Offer the section force on the tab: rejected, it is not a stress and the engineer would divide by the thickness in their head. Where it is computed: by the fea package, with its ends and display averages, not by the page dividing two numbers (ADR-0001). For the deformation: (1) a button for the total and one for each direction the model moves in, chosen; (2) a button along z on an in-plane model too, showing zeros: rejected, the tab says in a line that nothing moves along z. For the layout: (1) one longer row of fifteen buttons: rejected, the complaint was that the tab read as ply stresses only; (2) groups with a heading each, as a desktop program's results tree lists them: chosen.

**Decision.** The package writes `element.laminate_stress`, its display averages and its ranges `S_x`, `S_y` and `S_xy` (`recovery.laminate_stress`, `fields.field_data`; theory section 5). The viewer offers them as "Laminate stress σx", "σy" and "Laminate shear stress τxy", only where the result holds them, so a run from before this shows what it showed. The Results tab lists its fields in four groups (`TREE_GROUPS`, `components/workspace/results-tab.tsx`): Failure; Deformation (displacement), with the total and each of x, y and, where the model bends, z; Stress of the whole laminate, with a line that says what it is and that a ply's own stress is below; and One ply, in its fibre axes, with the ply to pick. The group names both words because one program says deformation and the other displacement. A clicked element's readout carries its laminate stress.

**Evidence.** `fea/tests/test_fields.py` ("the laminate stress is the mean of the plies through the thickness"): checked another way than it is computed, against the thickness-weighted mean of the plies' own stresses in the model's axes, for a pulled coupon and a bending one, where it is zero. On the public IM7/8552 \[45/0/-45/90]s coupon pulled 1 mm (`fea/tools/studio_tool.py live` on `tests/fixtures/studio/values.json`, 2026-11-25) the laminate stress along the coupon at the gauge centre is 443.515 MPa and the reaction over the cross-section is 16,222.04 N / (25 mm x 1.46304 mm) = 443.516 MPa. `tests/studio-fields.test.ts` (the options, values and ends are the package's; none on an older result) and `tests/browser/studio.spec.ts` (the four groups, the legend "Laminate stress σy (MPa)", the deformation along x and y, and no button along z on a coupon pulled in its plane). No evidence yet from the team that the mean stress is the reading they meant.
