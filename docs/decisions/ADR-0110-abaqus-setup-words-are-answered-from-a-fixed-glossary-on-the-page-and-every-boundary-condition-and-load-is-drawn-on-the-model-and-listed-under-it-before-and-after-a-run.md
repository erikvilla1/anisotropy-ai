## Context

- Engineers who set models up in Abaqus speak its words: boundary condition (BC), encastre, pinned, XSYMM, U2, UR1, CF3, seed, partition, step, job. The assistant knew none of them. "What is a boundary condition?" went to the why answers (ADR-0102), which need a result and a model; "add a boundary condition" on a coupon was read into nothing; on a plate the guard of ADR-0101 knew "fixed", "clamped" and "simply supported" only, so "YSYMM on the bottom edge" was left to a model to guess.
- The page called a coupon's boundary conditions and loads "grips" and its step "Loads and supports". The founder asked for the words of the analysis (quoted above) and uploaded a getting-started guide to Abaqus so that the assistant would know what an engineer means. The guide is Dassault's and says no part of it may be reproduced: it may be read, not copied.
- What the model view showed, found on 2026-11-18 by reading the code and by screenshots:
  - After a run `results-view.tsx` passed only `preview.loaded`, the first load, to the scene. A plate's second load, drawn before the run, was gone after it.
  - A held set was a frame around its nodes; which directions it held was said only in a key under the model. That key sat inside the canvas's box, which clips (`overflow: hidden`), so it was never seen. The sketch before the check had the same fault.
  - A pressure was one arrow at the face's centre. A plate's boundary conditions and loads proposed by the assistant were not drawn until applied.

## Options considered

1. **A fixed glossary in our own words, read by the page before anything else; the names with one meaning fixed in the plate guard; every input drawn on the model and listed under it, the same before and after a run (chosen).** No model is asked what a word means, and the answer is the same every time. The glossary is short and has to be kept by hand.
2. **Put the guide, or our summary of it, in the retrieval index of the why answers and let the model answer.** Rejected: the guide's text may not be reproduced; a word with one meaning needs no model, and a model call takes seconds and can say it differently each time; and a why answer needs a result, while a word is asked before there is one.
3. **The words only, the model view as it was.** Rejected: the founder's ask was that the inputs be shown, and a load lost after a run is a fault, not a choice.
4. **Map the Abaqus names on the page only, leaving the plate guard as it was.** Rejected: on a plate a model reads the message (ADR-0101), and the guard is where quoted words become directions. A name the guard does not know is a direction the model may get wrong.

## Decision

- **The glossary.** `lib/abaqus-terms.ts` holds 22 terms, each with the names an engineer uses, what it means in one short sentence and what Anisotropy does with it in another. A name with one exact meaning has its own sentence (U1 to UR3, XSYMM, YSYMM, ZSYMM, CF1 to CF3, RF1 to RF3). The sentences are ours: no sentence is copied from a manual and none names a program (ADR-0091); a test holds both.
- **What the chat does with a word.** Read by the page, first, with or without a result, before the why answers:
  - "what is a BC?", "what does encastre mean?", "what is U2?" is answered from the glossary: the word as the heading, its meaning, then what Anisotropy does with it.
  - "what are the boundary conditions?", "show the loads" (this model's own) lists the model's, one line each, as the list under the model says them.
  - "add a boundary condition", "partition the coupon", "create a step" says where it is done or that the setup already holds it, and changes nothing. A coupon's boundary conditions are set by its test, as in the test machine; a plate is held and loaded by naming a region (ADR-0101), so on a plate read by a model a boundary condition or a load goes on to be read into its lists.
  - "seed 2 mm", "global size 3" states the element size (ADR-0088 amendment 2).
- **The words on the page.** "Boundary conditions and loads" in place of "Loads and supports". A held set is said with its directions and, where Abaqus has a name for them, the name in brackets: "Bottom grip: fixed (encastre)", "Edge-Left: held along x, y, z (pinned)", "(YSYMM)". The plate panel labels each direction U1 to UR3 with its words as the title.
- **The model view.** One function (`sceneInputs`) gives the scene every boundary condition and every load of the preview, before a run and after it. A boundary condition is drawn at a few nodes spread over its set: a cone pointing at the node for each move held, a double cone for each turn held. A load's arrows are spread over its set, a pressure arriving from above the face it presses. On a plate, what the assistant proposes is drawn dashed until it is applied. Under the model a list says what is applied: Boundary conditions, Loads, Proposed (not applied yet), and the model they act on (material, layup, elements, one linear static step). A line clicked frames its set on the model. The list and the sketch's key sit outside the canvas's clipped box.

## Consequences

- An engineer who speaks Abaqus is understood for the words of a setup, and every input is on the model, so what is solved can be checked by eye before and after a run.
- The glossary is kept by hand. A word not in it is not answered from it; it goes on to the why answers or the patterns, as before.
- The scene draws more: a symbol per direction held at up to nine nodes per set, and up to nine arrows per load.

## How we will know it was wrong

- An engineer asks what a setup word means and the chat answers something else, or nothing, in the run records or the founder's tries: the glossary's names are wrong or too few.
- A boundary condition or load solved is missing from the model or the list, before or after a run, or the list says one the model did not solve.
- Engineers find the symbols hide the result: the drawing goes behind a switch.

## Amendment 1, 2026-11-18 (one glossary for every program's words)

**Context.** The founder, on the model tree of [ADR-0111](ADR-0111-the-fea-workspace-sets-a-model-up-in-a-model-tree-on-the-left-in-the-words-of-any-finite-element-program-and-the-chat-changes-the-same-tabs.md): "its not an abaqus style panel but more of an FEA style panel, because ansys will also get imported through our software". The glossary knew one program's names; an engineer who learnt another asks "what is a named selection?" or "fixed support".

**Options.** A glossary per program, chosen by the model's source: rejected, since a word asked before any model is loaded has no source, and two answers for one meaning would drift apart. One term per meaning holding every program's names for it (chosen).

**Decision.** `lib/abaqus-terms.ts` becomes `lib/fea-terms.ts` (`FEA_TERMS`, `FeaTerm`); each term also holds the names another program gives it: fixed support (encastre); frictionless support (roller); remote displacement (boundary condition); remote force and nodal force; line pressure; named selection and component (set); modeling ply, ply group and stackup (section and layup); rosette and oriented selection set (material orientation); body (part); static structural, analysis settings and load step (step); connection, bonded contact and joint (interaction); sizing, body, face, edge and element sizing (seed); SHELL181 and SHELL281 (element type); solution (job); force reaction (reaction force); UX to ROTZ, each with its one meaning. A program named after the word ("in Ansys", "in Abaqus/CAE") is read off it. "Simply supported" is left out: the plate guard reads it as held in z (ADR-0101), not as pinned. The sentences are unchanged except the degree of freedom's, which names both sets of labels.

**Evidence.** `tests/fea-terms.test.ts` (renamed), 6 pass: the five of this record, and a new one asking "fixed support", "frictionless support", "named selection in Ansys", "edge sizing", "static structural", "remote displacement", "modeling ply", "rosette", "force reaction", "UY" and "ROTZ", and adding "a fixed support" and "a remote force". UNVERIFIED: no engineer has asked one of these names.
